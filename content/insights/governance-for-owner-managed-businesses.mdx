Owners of successful private companies often hear that they "need governance" and picture committees, charters and a slower business. Done badly, that is exactly what they get. Done well, governance is simply the discipline of making sure the right decisions are taken by the right people, with the right information, and that someone is checking.

## Four building blocks

1. **Clarity of roles.** Who owns the business, who directs it and who manages it day to day. In owner-managed companies these are often the same people, which is fine, provided the hats are worn consciously.
2. **Delegation of authority.** A one-page matrix that says who can commit the company to what, and above which threshold a second signature or board approval is needed.
3. **A policy framework that fits.** Five to ten short policies on the matters that genuinely carry risk: approvals, procurement, conflicts of interest, information security, people.
4. **Oversight.** A regular, structured review of performance, risks and compliance with a written record of decisions.

## Signs your governance is not keeping up

- Decisions are made in corridors and remembered differently by different people.
- Managers are unsure what they are allowed to approve.
- The same issue is discussed at three consecutive meetings without a decision.
- The board pack is 80 pages and nobody reads page 40.
- Nobody can say when policies were last reviewed.

## What a health check covers

A governance health check looks at structures, responsibilities, authority, policies, oversight and reporting, and rates each against good practice for an organisation of your size. The output is usually a short list of practical improvements rather than a wholesale redesign.

> Proportionate governance makes a business easier to run, easier to finance and easier to hand over.

## Start small

If you do nothing else this quarter: write the delegation of authority matrix, agree a one-page management report, and schedule a quarterly review with minutes. Those three steps address most of the governance gaps we see.
