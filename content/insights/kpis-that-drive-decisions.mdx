If your monthly report has 40 indicators, you have 40 numbers and no priorities. The goal of a KPI set is to tell management, quickly, whether the organisation is on track and where to intervene.

## The decision test

For each candidate indicator ask: **if this number moved significantly, what would we do differently?** If there is no clear answer, it is interesting data, not a key performance indicator.

## A simple structure

Organise indicators around what the business must get right:

- **Customers**: retention, satisfaction, complaints resolved on time.
- **Operations**: on-time delivery, quality, utilisation.
- **Finance**: gross margin by line, cash conversion, overdue receivables.
- **People**: vacancies, turnover in critical roles, training completed.
- **Risk and compliance**: open high risks, overdue actions, compliance breaches.

Three to five indicators per area is plenty. Each needs a definition, a data source, an owner and a target.

## Leading versus lagging

Revenue is lagging: by the time it falls, the cause is months old. Pipeline conversion, quote turnaround and customer complaints are leading: they move first. A useful KPI set mixes both.

## Make the report earn its place

- One page for the leadership team, with trend and target, not just this month.
- A short commentary from each owner: what happened, why, what we are doing.
- A standing agenda item where indicators off target get a decision, not a discussion.

## Common traps

- Measuring what the system makes easy rather than what matters.
- Setting targets without agreeing the definition first.
- Changing indicators every quarter so no trend ever builds.
- Treating the dashboard as the output; the decision is the output.
