Most management teams are good at reacting to symptoms. The month-end close slips, so finance works the weekend. A regulator writes in, so someone is asked to "sort out compliance". A customer complains, so the operations manager apologises and moves on.

Each response is reasonable. None of them asks the more useful question: **why did this happen, and what else is it telling us?**

## Symptoms travel across functions

In our experience, a problem that surfaces in one area almost always originates somewhere else:

- A **financial** issue, such as unreliable margins, often starts with a **process** weakness in how costs are captured.
- A **compliance** failure usually reflects unclear **accountability**: nobody owned the obligation, so nobody tracked it.
- An **operational** problem, like repeated stock-outs, frequently exposes a wider **control or governance** gap in how decisions are made and reviewed.

When these issues are handled separately, by separate people, the organisation ends up with three fixes and no improvement.

## What a 360° perspective looks like in practice

Taking a 360° perspective does not mean reviewing everything at once. It means looking at how six elements interact before deciding what to change:

1. **Strategy**: what the organisation is trying to achieve, and whether that is understood.
2. **People**: roles, accountability and capacity.
3. **Processes**: how work actually flows, including the workarounds.
4. **Performance**: what is measured, reported and acted upon.
5. **Risks**: what could prevent objectives from being met.
6. **Controls**: what is in place to prevent or detect problems early.

A late month-end, viewed this way, might reveal that approvals are concentrated in one person (people), that purchase orders are raised after invoices arrive (process), that nobody reports ageing accruals (performance) and that there is no authority limit for commitments (controls). Fixing the close without addressing those will only buy a few weeks.

## Three questions to ask before fixing anything

> Before you fix the symptom, ask: Where did this start? Who owns it? What would have told us earlier?

If you cannot answer all three, the fix will not hold.

## Where to start

A structured diagnostic across the six elements typically takes a few weeks and produces a short, prioritised list of improvements. It is far cheaper than a year of firefighting, and it gives the leadership team a shared picture of the business rather than six separate ones.
